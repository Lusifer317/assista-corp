Engagement flow

Start with clarity. Build a reliable working rhythm.

A good engagement makes the work, systems, handoffs, and approval rules clear before day-to-day execution begins. Explore the typical stages below.

Understand the working rhythm

Map recurring responsibilities, decision points, communication preferences, stakeholders, and the work that should stay with the executive.

  • Priority rules
  • Meeting cadence
  • Escalation contacts

Define access and decision rights

Agree which calendars, inbox folders, files, and systems are in scope. Define what can be scheduled, drafted, or updated—and what needs approval.

  • Named user access
  • Least-privilege permissions
  • Approval and escalation rules

Start with a focused work lane

Begin with a small set of repeatable activities, documented instructions, and a review cadence before expanding the scope.

  • Task checklist
  • Working instructions
  • Review checkpoints

Review and refine

Use regular check-ins to identify bottlenecks, missed handoffs, and tasks that can safely be standardized or expanded.

  • Open action review
  • Exceptions and blockers
  • Scope adjustments
Operating boundaries

Clear responsibilities protect the work.

Support is most effective when the team knows what it can prepare or execute, what needs review, and what must stay with the client. These boundaries should be written into the engagement scope.

What support can cover

Coordination, preparation, organization, reminders, approved drafting, and administrative follow-through within an agreed scope.

What stays with your leadership

Final business decisions, sensitive relationship commitments, contract approvals, financial authorization, hiring decisions, and any message requiring executive judgment.

Operational visibility

Choose measures that reflect the work—not vanity metrics.

The measures below are examples to agree at kickoff. Baselines, definitions, targets, data sources, and reporting cadence should be set for each engagement.

Follow-throughOpen action items, overdue items, and closure against agreed due dates.
Meeting readinessPreparation completeness, agenda readiness, and missing pre-read follow-ups.
Calendar qualityScheduling rework, avoidable conflicts, and focus time protected against agreed rules.

No response-time, savings, placement, or productivity target is implied by this page. Any service level is agreed only after workload and coverage are understood.

Before you enquire

Is this support model right for your team?

The strongest starting point is a repeatable process with a clear owner, an agreed definition of done, and a review path for exceptions.

Often a good fit when

A good fit when leaders have recurring coordination work, clear preferences, and tasks that can be delegated with review rules.

What to bring to the first conversation

Bring a typical week, the tools involved, the tasks you want delegated first, and examples of decisions that should always come back to you.

Frequently asked questions

Questions worth answering upfront.

Expand a question to review the practical considerations for this service.

Can an assistant send messages on my behalf?

That depends on message type and approval rules. A prudent starting point is triage and draft preparation, with sensitive or commitment-making messages held for your review.

Can you manage calendars across time zones?

Calendar coordination can be scoped around participants, working hours, scheduling rules, and tools. Specific coverage windows are agreed before work begins.

How do you handle sensitive access?

Access should be provisioned through named accounts, least-privilege permissions, MFA where available, and an agreed escalation path. Do not share personal passwords.

Can the service expand later?

Start with a defined work lane, review the results, then agree any additional tasks, systems, or coverage in writing.
